Lesson 3 · 计算番数

Scoring & Fan

A win is not just a win. Every winning hand is priced by its fan 番 — pattern bonuses that stack on top of each other — and the price decides who pays you what. This lesson walks from a finished hand to the exact number of points that changes hands.

1. From a hand to a number

Two steps, in this order:

  1. Count the fan — add up every pattern your hand qualifies for. Fan is additive: a hand does not "become" a better pattern, it collects all of them at once.
  2. Convert to points — 分 = 番 × 底分. The base value 底分 is a table convention; on this site it is 1, so one fan = one point and the fan count is also the point count.

Stacking is where the money is

A plain hand wins 1 point. A plain hand won on your own draw in a single suit wins 6, and if it also contains two four-of-a-kinds it wins 8. Beginners count the shape; good players count the stack.

2. The fan table

Hand-shape fan

PatternChineseFanCondition
Base win平胡+1Any standard win — four sets plus a pair. The floor, always counted.
All triplets大对子 碰碰胡+2All four sets are triplets (or kongs). No runs anywhere.
All one suit清一色+4Every tile in the hand, melds included, is from a single suit. The big one.
Four of a kind根+1 eachPer tile you hold four of. Counted in every hand shape, including seven pairs.
Seven pairs七对+2Seven distinct pairs, no melds exposed. Replaces the base win.
Dragon seven pairs龙七对+2Seven pairs that contain a four-of-a-kind. Added on top of 七对.
All kongs十八罗汉+2All four of your melds are kongs — a legendary shape most players never see.

Situational fan

BonusChineseFanCondition
Self-draw自摸+1You drew the winning tile yourself rather than taking a discard.
Kong blossom杠上花+1The winning tile was the replacement tile drawn after a kong, and you drew it yourself.

Note that 自摸 and 杠上花 stack: a self-drawn win on a kong replacement is +1 +1. Situational fan is added after the shape fan, so a self-drawn all-one-suit hand is 1 + 4 + 1 = 6, not 4.

4. Who pays

How you wonWho paysHow much each
Self-draw 自摸Every player who has not yet won this roundThe full fan value, each
Off a discard 点炮Only the player who discarded that tileThe full fan value, alone

Two consequences worth internalising. First, self-draw is worth roughly three times a discard win, because three people pay instead of one — which is why 自摸 is worth chasing. Second, because this is battle to the end 血战到底, "who has not yet won" shrinks as the round goes on: the third winner is paid by one player, not three.

5. Kong money is paid on the spot

Kong points do not wait for the end of the round. They change hands the moment the kong is made:

Kong typeChineseWho paysEach pays
Exposed kong (from a discard)直杠The player who discarded the 4th tile2
Concealed kong (all four in hand)暗杠All three opponents2
Added kong (on your own pong)补杠All three opponents1

The refund clause 退税

Kong money is only yours if you eventually win the round. A player who collects kong points and never wins has to give every point back at settlement. This is the single most surprising rule for newcomers — and it is why a kong is a commitment, not free money.

6. When the wall runs out

If the round ends without three winners, nobody wins and penalties are charged instead:

SettlementChineseWhat happens
Ready check查叫 查大叫 Every player who is not ready (下叫) pays each ready player the fan value of that player's best wait — so the better your wait, the more you collect.
Flower pig查花猪 Anyone still holding tiles of their declared missing suit pays 16 points to each player who cleared theirs. The most expensive mistake in the game.
Tax refund退税 Everyone who never won refunds all the kong money they collected.

Read those three together and the game's whole incentive structure appears: clear your missing suit, get ready (下叫), and keep your wait as fat as possible. Almost every strategic idea in Sichuan mahjong is downstream of that sentence.

7. Where these rules come from

One documented preset, not the only one

Sichuan mahjong fan values genuinely differ from table to table and city to city — all-one-suit is worth 2 in some places and 4 in others, and rare fan names shift too. All-triplets is normally 大对子 on a Sichuan table (the rest of China says 碰碰胡), and the four-kong hand here is named 十八罗汉 — some tables reserve 金钩钓 for a different and far more common shape, every meld exposed with a single-tile wait.

Where a name is genuinely contested, this site uses the term its own engine computes and says so, rather than quietly teaching one region's vocabulary as if it were universal.

Every number on this page is exactly what the table and the calculator on this site will compute, so the lesson and the scores can never disagree. Treat it as one clearly documented preset — then check your local table's house rules before you play for anything that matters.
