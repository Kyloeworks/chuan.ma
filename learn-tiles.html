Lesson 2 · 识别牌面

Reading the Tiles

You cannot play a game you cannot read. The good news: Sichuan mahjong has the smallest tile set of any major variant — 108 tiles, three suits, nine numbers. Below is every face, drawn at the size you will actually see across a table. Learn nine characters, four colour landmarks and one visual trap, and you can name any tile on the table in under a second.

1. A deliberately stripped deck

If you have seen Hong Kong or Japanese mahjong, you are used to extra tiles: winds 東南西北, dragons 中發白, flowers and seasons. Sichuan mahjong throws all of them away.

What is left is three suits of nine ranks, four copies of each tile:

SuitChinesePinyinWhat you actually seeMnemonic
Characters / myriads万wàn a Chinese numeral sitting above the character 萬 万 means "ten thousand" — the money suit
Bamboo / sticks条tiáo that many bamboo canes drawn on the tile 条 means "strip" or "stick"
Dots / circles筒tǒng that many circles drawn on the tile 筒 means "tube" or "barrel"

3 suits × 9 ranks × 4 copies = 108 tiles. That is the entire game.

Why the deck is small, and why that matters

With no winds or dragons to get in the way, almost every hand is made of the same raw material: runs and triplets inside three suits. Sichuan mahjong is therefore much purer than other variants — it is almost entirely a test of shape efficiency. See the basics for how that plays out.

2. All 27 faces, at table size

This is the whole vocabulary of the game on one screen. Nothing is missing and nothing is added — if you can name these, you can read any hand.

Notice how differently the three suits encode a number. Characters write it. Bamboo and dots count it — which is why bamboo and dots are the fast suits to read, and characters are the ones worth drilling.

4. The one tile that is not what it looks like

1 Bamboo 一条 is drawn as a bird

Every other bamboo tile shows its rank as a count of canes — except the 1. Traditionally it is drawn as a bird (a peacock or sparrow), not as a single cane. Players call it 幺鸡 (yāo jī, literally "little chicken").

So if you are scanning a hand for "one bamboo", stop looking for a lone stick. The bird is the one. This single piece of tile art has confused more beginners than any rule in the game.

Three things to read on the bird, in the order you will actually notice them: the red crest on its head, the long plumes sweeping down to the left, and the small red knot where the tail leaves the body — the last one is a fossil. The tile started life as a drawing of a string of coins (一百钱 to a string) tied with a red knot; over time the knot was read as a bird's head and the coins as its body, and the knot simply stayed there.

5. Four bamboo tiles you can read by colour alone

Bamboo tiles are not a plain grid of identical canes. Real sets colour specific canes red, and lay the eight out unlike any other rank — so four of the nine bamboo tiles can be identified without counting a single cane. Most players discover this at a table, under time pressure, the hard way.

5 条the middle cane is red
7 条the top cane is red
9 条the whole middle row is red
8 条two upright canes, one mountain between

Three of them are pure colour: a red dot in the centre (5), a red dot on top (7), a red band across the middle (9). Nine bamboo is the easiest tile in the deck to spot from across a room, precisely because of that band.

Eight bamboo is a shape instead. It is the only tile in the game whose sides are two upright canes — everything else is a grid. Between them sit two shorter, steeply slanted canes meeting in the middle: the top half forms a ∧, the bottom half a ∨, mirrored, with a gap left between them. Its nicknames at Chinese tables are unforgiving: 坦克 (a tank, seen from above), 脚开开 (legs wide open), and BMW — B for 八, M for the bottom half, W for the top.

Why colour beats counting

Counting nine canes costs a beat of conscious attention; seeing a red band costs none. Under time pressure that difference is the whole game — but note the caveat: red canes are a convention, not a law. Cheap or unusual sets sometimes omit them. Use colour as your fast cue, and counting as the fallback that always works.

6. Saying a tile out loud

The pattern is always [number][suit] — number first, suit second. That is the opposite of how English speakers tend to guess.

FaceTileChinesePinyinMeans
三万三 + 万sān wàn3 of characters
七条七 + 条qī tiáo7 of bamboo
五筒五 + 筒wǔ tǒng5 of dots
幺鸡幺 + 鸡yāo jī1 of bamboo (the bird)
九万九 + 万jiǔ wàn9 of characters

Chinese mahjong tables are loud partly because discards are announced: you say the tile name as you throw it. Learn these five phrases and you can follow the conversation at a real table.

A quick naming convention you will hear constantly: because the rank comes first, a hand sorted from 1 to 9 in one suit reads as a list of numbers — 幺鸡, 二条, 三条 and so on. Sorted hands are also announced by their shape, e.g. "I have 1-2-3 bamboo", which is how you will start hearing your own hands.

7. The real skill: sorting a hand fast

Naming tiles is table stakes. What actually makes you a player is the first three seconds of every round: sorting 13 tiles into their three suits and seeing which suit is thinnest.

  1. Split into three piles — characters left, bamboo middle, dots right (or whatever order you like, as long as it never changes).
  2. Order each pile 1 → 9 so runs and pairs jump out at you.
  3. Count each pile. The smallest pile is your natural missing suit 定缺 — the suit you will throw away entirely this round.

Doing this reflexively is worth more than any strategy tip on this site. If you have to think about it, you will lose the first three turns of every round.

8. Drills you can do right here

Both drills use exactly the tile faces from the table. Click to answer; the feedback tells you what you actually picked, and flags the classic mix-ups.

Drill A · Name the tile 认牌

One tile at a time — sometimes face down at first. Name it out loud before you look at the options.

Drill B · Call the missing suit 定缺

Thirteen tiles dealt from a real wall. Count the three suits and drop the thinnest — in one motion, without pausing.

Drill C · Speed sort

Deal yourself 13 real tiles and sort them into three ordered suits. Target: under 10 seconds, no mistakes. You can do it on the tile gallery, which shows every face at several sizes, or on the calculator, which lets you click tiles in and immediately tells you what the hand is worth.
