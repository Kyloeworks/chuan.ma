Glossary

Every term used on this site, in English and Chinese, with pinyin. Tiles and discards are announced in Chinese at a real table, so the pinyin column is the one worth learning first.

Actions at the table

EnglishChinesePinyinWhat it means
Draw摸牌mō páiTake a tile from the wall.
Discard打牌dǎ páiThrow a tile face-up into your discard pool.
Pong碰pèngClaim a discard to make three identical tiles, exposed.
Kong杠gàngFour identical tiles. Three variants, all scored differently.
Exposed kong直杠zhí gàngKong made by claiming a discarded 4th tile; the discarder pays 2.
Concealed kong暗杠àn gàngKong held entirely in your own hand; every opponent pays 2.
Added kong补杠bǔ gàngAdding a drawn 4th tile to your own exposed pong; each opponent pays 1.
Win胡húComplete a legal hand.
Self-drawn win自摸zì mōWin on a tile you drew yourself. Three players pay instead of one.
Declare missing suit定缺dìng quēName one suit you will discard entirely this round. Mandatory, public, before the first discard.
Chow吃chīClaiming a discard to complete a run. Not used in Sichuan mahjong — listed here so you know what is missing.

Hand shapes & patterns

EnglishChinesePinyinWhat it means
Pair将jiàngThe two identical tiles that complete a hand.
Triplet刻子kè ziThree identical tiles, in hand or exposed.
Run顺子shùn ziThree consecutive tiles in one suit.
Four of a kind根gēnHolding all four copies of a tile. Worth +1 fan each — except in seven-pairs hands, where 龙七对 already pays for it.
Seven pairs七对qī duìSeven distinct pairs, no melds exposed. Worth 4, replacing the base win.
Dragon seven pairs龙七对lóng qī duìSeven pairs containing a four-of-a-kind. Worth 8 in total.
All triplets大对子dà duì ziEvery set is a triplet or kong — no runs. Worth 3. Exposed pongs and kongs still count. Known as 碰碰胡 outside Sichuan.
Golden hook金钩钓jīn gōu diàoAll four sets exposed as pongs or kongs, leaving just a pair in hand waiting on one tile. Worth 3.
All one suit清一色qīng yī sèEvery tile in one suit. +4 — or already inside one of the pure 清 packages (清对 8, 清七对 16, 清龙七对 32). The big money pattern.
Terminals everywhere带幺九dài yāo jiǔEvery set and the pair contains a 1 or a 9. +2.
No terminals at all断幺九duàn yāo jiǔNo 1s or 9s anywhere. A national-standard / Japanese pattern, not a Sichuan one — shipped here as a switch, off by default.
All kongs十八罗汉shí bā luó hànAll four melds are kongs — 18 tiles. Worth 16, and rarer than you will ever see. Distinct from 金钩钓, which is four exposed melds of any kind with a single-tile wait.
Robbing the kong抢杠胡qiǎng gàng húWinning on the fourth tile someone was adding to their pong. The kong is void, no kong money is paid, and you get +1.

Reading your own hand

EnglishChinesePinyinWhat it means
Ready下叫xià jiàoOne tile away from winning. This is what a Sichuan table actually says; 听牌 is the standard-Mandarin equivalent you will see in books.
Wait听张tīng zhāngThe specific tile(s) that would complete your hand.
Distance to ready差几张下叫chà jǐ zhāng xià jiàoHow many tiles you are still away from ready. A Sichuan player says "still 2 tiles from 下叫"; 向听 is a Japanese-mahjong loanword and is not used at a Sichuan table.
Improving tiles进张jìn zhāngThe tiles that would move you one step closer. More improving tiles = better hand.
Discard pool牌河pái héThe face-up piles of discards in front of each player — public information.
Wall牌墙pái qiángThe undealt tiles. When it empties, the round settles.
Your hand手牌shǒu páiThe tiles you are holding.
Exposed melds副露fù lùPongs and kongs laid face-up in front of you.

Scoring & settlement

EnglishChinesePinyinWhat it means
Fan番fānThe pattern bonus value of a hand. Bonuses stack.
Points分fēnWhat actually changes hands: fan × base value.
Base value底分dǐ fēnThe multiplier applied to fan. Set to 1 on this site.
Kong blossom杠上花gàng shàng huāSelf-drawn win on the replacement tile drawn after a kong. +1.
Kong cannon杠上炮gàng shàng pàoWinning off the tile someone was forced to discard right after their kong. +1.
Last tile海底hǎi dǐA win on the very last tile in the wall. +1.
Ready check查叫chá jiàoWall ran out: players who are not ready pay each ready player the fan value of their best wait. Often written 查大叫.
Flower pig查花猪chá huā zhūWall ran out: anyone still holding their declared missing suit pays 16 to each clean player.
Tax refund退税tuì shuìKong money collected by a player who never wins is handed back.

Tiles, table & culture

EnglishChinesePinyinWhat it means
Mahjong麻将má jiàngThe game. Tiles are 牌 (pái).
Sichuan mahjong四川麻将 / 川麻sì chuān má jiàng / chuān máThe variant taught on this site. Also just chuan ma.
Characters suit万wàn"Ten thousand" — the money suit, ranks written as numerals.
Bamboo suit条tiáo"Stick" — drawn as that many bamboo sticks.
Dots suit筒tǒng"Barrel" — drawn as that many circles.
1 of bamboo幺鸡yāo jīLiterally "little chicken" — the 1 of bamboo is drawn as a bird, not a stick.
Dealer庄家zhuāng jiāThe player who acts first.
Teahouse茶馆chá guǎnWhere Sichuan mahjong actually lives — the game is inseparable from teahouse culture.
Battle to the end血战到底xuè zhàn dào dǐThe standard variant: after a win the round continues until three players have won.
Blood flows like a river血流成河xuè liú chéng héThe extreme variant: winners keep playing and can win repeatedly.

The five you will hear in the first minute at a real table

定缺 dìng quē (declare your missing suit) · 下叫 xià jiào (I'm ready) · 碰 pèng (pong) · 杠 gàng (kong) · 自摸 zì mō (self-drawn win) · 幺鸡 yāo jī (the 1 of bamboo). Learn those six and you can follow a table well enough to learn the rest by ear.
